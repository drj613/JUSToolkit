## 2. Item 1: the ability bitset is NOT where resistance lives

`docs/research/Ability-Bitset-Is-Not-Resistance.md`.

The cached ability bitset at `entity + 0x128` is real and **is** read during combat —
but it does not scale damage. Both directions were tested, each on a single target to
avoid cross-character confounds:

- **Setting** bit 9 (`0x09` blunt resistance) on an ability-free dummy: no change. All
  32 bits were swept individually; only bit 4 (`0x04` オートガード Auto-Guard) did
  anything, and it drove damage to **zero**.
- **Clearing** bit 9 on a real resistor (Luffy): **352 raw in all 12 runs**, delta
  +0.000, with two controls also flat.

The bit-4 result is what makes those nulls trustworthy: a single bit flip swings damage
from 6.0 to 0.0, so the instrument is demonstrably live. The baseline was re-measured
six times across the 32-bit sweep and read 384 every time.

**The flat −2 is not ability `0x09` via any runtime read.** The confound flagged in
`Damage-Reduction-Is-Flat.md` is now the leading explanation: a per-character defence
value, or a stat derived at load and stored elsewhere. The best next lead is atlas's
`record+0x3C` low nibble used as an ignore mask, plus the community guide's claim of
three separate resistance categories (punch/kick, special, blade).

## 3. Item 2: navigation rebuilt from pixels

`docs/research/Menu-Nav-Verified-From-Pixels.md`, and the first attempt's failure in

`boot_verified.py` reaches a verified training battle in **3/3 cold boots** with items
and gimmicks switched off. Two things were wrong with the old route, both found by
looking rather than reasoning:

- The top menu is a **4x2 icon grid** whose cursor sat on デッキメイク, so the old
  "one RIGHT from Jギャラクシー" walked into the deck editor. That is where the mystery
  編集/コピー submenu came from.
- The grid **wraps**, so saturating to a corner also fails: UP x3 + LEFT x5 landed on
  オプション.

Navigation now taps absolute touchscreen coordinates and **verifies the source screen
before every press**. That verification is the real fix — pressing mid-transition is
what swallowed inputs.

Verified route (DS bottom-screen coordinates):

| from | action | to |
|---|---|---|
| title | START, repeated until recognised (took 4-6) | `top_menu` |
| `top_menu` | tap (93, 49) Jアリーナ, then A | `arena_menu` |
| `arena_menu` | tap (180, 142) トレーニング, then A | `deck_select` |
| `deck_select` | A | `stage_select` |
| `stage_select` | A | `rule_select` |
| `rule_select` | items+gimmicks off, then START | `battle` |

**A design that made things worse, recorded so nobody retries it:** trying candidate
actions per step with fallbacks dropped reliability from 2/3 to **0/3**. A failed
attempt is not side-effect-free — a speculative START pushed the game somewhere the
fallback could not recover from. With stateful input, waiting beats guessing. Toggles
are the exception: they are reversible and can be checked after each tap.

## 4. Item 3: deck editor — what works and what does not

`scripts/emu/deck_editor.py` holds the verified constants and, deliberately, the open
questions.

**Works:** route to the editor (`top_menu` → tap (33, 96) デッキメイク → tap deck slot
→ tap (208, 58) 編集); **X** brings the canvas down to the touch screen (fingerprint
+69.35) while Y does not (+1.42); the clear-deck tray button at **(248, 88)** empties
the deck (canvas went to a clean 5x4 grid, 107 bytes changed); the series filter
applies when the focused DRAGON BALL icon at **(23, 111)** is tapped (+81.68, list
became 悟空 / 超サイヤ人悟空 / ベジット).

**The RAM oracle matters more than pixels here.** Deck state lives at
`0x020A0C00`–`0x020B0000`. Diffing it across an action gives a definitive "did the deck
change" that the screen cannot: a canvas tap moved the bottom-screen fingerprint by
**0.33** while changing **121 bytes** of deck state. Idle drift is **18 bytes** — that
is the noise floor.

**Placement, per the owner:** tapping the target cell alone works, as do tap-then-A,
tap-dpad-A and A-dpad-A. No dragging.

**Helpers need a direction.** After placing a helper koma you must point it at a battle
character to say who gets its passive; the only exception is a +1 SP helper, since
SP is shared. This explains a confusing early result — the first attempt used セナ's
1-koma, which is ヘルプ, so it changed 1097 bytes, left the cell looking empty and put
a marker on an already-placed koma. That was an incomplete helper placement, not a
failed one. Use a バトル koma to test the basic flow.

**Placement CONFIRMED, and the missing piece was a second tap.** A koma row needs two
taps, exactly like the deck slots and the rule toggles: the first highlights, the
second selects and brings the canvas down. One tap alone looks like nothing happened
(fingerprint moved 3.96), which misled an earlier attempt.

Measured on 悟空's 4-koma (バトル) after filtering to Dragon Ball:

| step | fingerprint | effect |
|---|---|---|
| tap koma row, 1st | 3.96 | highlight only |
| tap koma row, 2nd | 64.59 | canvas comes down, koma preview floating |
| tap canvas cell | 22.41 | placed; deck state +212 bytes |

Confirmed by both signals: a canvas screenshot showing the 悟空 koma in the top-left
cell, and a deck-state diff well clear of the 18-byte noise floor.
`deck_editor.place_koma()` wraps this.

Row geometry was re-measured: row 1 centre is at DS y≈31 with ≈16px spacing (an earlier
guess of 20 was wrong).

Also unknown: what opens the series filter panel. A tap at (7, 8) on the 作 header and

## 6. Addresses and layout

**Session-local — always re-derive.** The character array moved between
`0x021DF1B4`, `0x021DF1D4` and `0x021DF204` across battles this session.

How to derive an entity (the handoff arithmetic from the previous session is wrong):

1. `char_struct = hp_block − 0x18`. Cross-check: the chr_b index sits at `hp+0x29` and
   at `char+0x41`, and `0x41 − 0x29 = 0x18`. RAM also holds many live pointers to
   `char_struct`, so it is a real object boundary.
2. Scan RAM for u32 pointers to `char_struct + 0x10`; the object holding one is the
   entity.

**`entity + 0x56C = char_struct` does NOT hold** — applied to this battle it lands
inside the *player's* deck array. It was probably a pointer load, not a subtraction.

| thing | value (this session) |
|---|---|
| ability bitset | `entity + 0x128`, u32, bit = ability ID |
| Goku entity / bitset | `0x022286E0` / `0x02228808` = `0x00008080` (abilities 7, 15) |
| dummy chr_b[70] entity / bitset | `0x0224E1E0` / `0x0224E308` = `0x00000000` |
| Luffy entity / bitset | `0x022441E0` / `0x02244308` = `0x02005200` (abilities 9, 25, 12, 14) |
| deck state region | `0x020A0C00`–`0x020B0000` |
| overlay load windows | `0x0214CD20` ov00-09, `0x02172A60` ov10-11, `0x021AC1C0` ov12-13 |

`entity + 0x04` and `+0x08` hold identical code pointers across characters
(`0x0215D3B4`, `0x0215D530`). They identify the object type but are **function
pointers, not a vtable** — atlas corrected this; `0x0215D3B4` is a 300-byte ARM
function.

Overlay residency, both screens pixel-verified
(`docs/research/Overlay-Residency-Deck-Screens.md`):

| screen | ov01 | ov05 |
|---|---|---|
| deck-make list (`DeckSelect.cpp`) | **99.6%** | 8.0% |
| deck editor (`KomaEdit`) | 4.8% | **99.5%** |

This closed atlas's long-standing `ov05_mode_UNIDENTIFIED`. The old run that reported
ov01 at 99.8% on "the deck editor" was measuring correctly but labelling wrongly — the
broken nav had landed it on the deck select list.

## 9. Standing rules

- **Doc voice.** Every research doc goes through `claude -p --model claude-opus-4-6` to
  be rewritten in Opus 4.6's voice before committing. Verify afterwards that no numbers
  or addresses were altered — grep for the load-bearing ones; the rewrite does convert
  hyphens to en-dashes in ranges.
- **Convergent verification.** For any load-bearing address, encoding or formula, find a
  second method that approaches it through a *different representation*. Agreement
  across representations cannot be shared bias. Example from this session: the ability
  bitset was located both by scanning for pointers to `char_struct+0x10` and by
  searching for the value `0x02005200` predicted from Luffy's ability IDs before
  looking — one match in 4MB. Stronger still, atlas's static layout left an 8-byte hole
  between `char+0x120` and `char+0x130`, and the bitset occupies exactly
  `+0x128`–`+0x12F`.
- **Run the independent checker BEFORE concluding**, not after. Codex confirming your
  conclusion is worth much less than Codex finding the inconsistency. It caught a
  mis-transcribed halfword this way.
- **Keep context lean**; prefer targeted greps and background runs writing to files over
  reading whole files. (Note: this session's instructions forbade using the Agent tool
  unless the user asked, so subagent delegation was not available. Worth clarifying with
  the owner.)

## 10. Hazards

- **melonDS hangs on savestate load, intermittently.** Happened twice this session, both
  times right after "Resetting JIT block cache". The process stays alive and the window
  keeps showing its last frame, so it *looks* fine — only the bridge framecount reveals
  it, and commands then fail with the INDETERMINATE no-ack error. A 36-load sweep ran
  clean while a 12-load experiment hung twice. Use `emu_health.ensure_alive(slot)`.
- **An unchanging screenshot is never evidence the game did not respond.** Confirm
  liveness from the bridge (framecount, or a RAM diff) first. This cost real time: the
  old macOS capture path returned stale frames for an occluded window while RAM changed
  by 13,520 bytes over 180 frames.
- **Never match a window by title.** The old capture code matched "melonDS" against
  window titles and captured the *terminal*, which was titled "Hand off melonDS runtime
  harness for JUS". Match the owning process.
- **`in_battle()` false-positives on the deck select screen.** A deck roster is HP values
  plus chr_b indices in `0x50`-byte slots — exactly the signature it scans for. It
  returned `0x02291574` with hp 10240 (160.0) while a deck menu was on screen. Always
  confirm a battle with pixels *and* RAM.
- **`functions.json` mis-bins merged functions** and `jus_files/analysis/disasm/ov6.txt`
  mis-decodes Thumb regions. Confirm any address with atlas before breakpointing.
- **Overlays share load addresses**, so an address in those windows means nothing
  without naming the resident overlay.
- **`DOWN+B` IS "Forced Change"** — confirmed by the owner. It makes the opponent swap
  battle character, and *every* battle character has it; it is universal, not part of
  anyone's kit. It is **not** a "special" the way the X moves are — it is an ordinary
  attack that carries a switch effect, so it has no reason to use a different damage
  pipeline. That matters because `DOWN+B` is the second move in
  `Damage-Reduction-Is-Flat.md`'s two-move proof, which only works if both moves hit the
  same reduction step. The flat conclusion stands. The residual gap: neither move used
  was side-effect-free, so re-running with a plain ordinary attack would close it —
  `UP+B` is a poor candidate, being a multi-hit string (1.0, 2.0, 1.0, 1.0).
- **GDB stub survives exactly one connection per launch.** Plan the whole session before
  attaching.
- **Blank frames are legitimate** early in ROM boot; treat a no-contrast capture as "not
  yet" while polling, not as an error.

## 11. The guide-derived experiment list

The session cross-referenced the 2007 GameFAQs community guide against this project's
research and produced **23 new experiment ideas, numbered 30–52**, in
`scratchpad/guide-derived-experiments.md`. None are code-verified; treat them as
hypotheses. The most interesting:

- **Per-move Attack Nature.** Each move may carry its own nature, separate from the
  character's koma nature, with the 1.5x multiplier keyed to the *attack's* nature
  versus the defender's. That would fit with "nature does not affect damage" if earlier
  tests varied character nature while the formula reads move nature. Atlas checked
  `0x0216A944` and found the right *shape* (two 4-bit fields, attacker value vs target
  fields) but concluded it is a team/side filter — matching values remove the target
  from the projectile spawn set, which deletes targets rather than scaling damage. Not
  ruled out; they are now looking on the damage-delta path from `0x02078488` /
  `0x020783CC`.
- **Three resistance categories** rather than one, which fits the `record+0x3C` ignore
  mask.
- **Three universal inputs, two still unmapped:** `down+B` Forced Change (now confirmed
  by the owner), `down+Y` Guard Break,
  `down+X` Push Attack.
- **16+ status effects** with one positive and one negative slot at a time, a new one
  overwriting.
